I design and teach undergraduate courses in comparative politics, with a focus on helping students build structured ways of analysing political systems, institutional change, and strategic behaviour. A core emphasis is developing students’ ability to think critically and evaluate competing explanations, drawing regularly on my own research and datasets in the classroom so students work with real, original data rather than only textbook examples.


## Democratisation from a Comparative Perspective

- SoSe 2025 and 2026
- Explores how and why political systems democratise, or fail to do so, across different historical and regional contexts. The course emphasises comparing theoretical frameworks and applying them to real-world cases.

## Democratic Development and Stability in Germany

- WiSe 23/24, 24/25, 25/26, and 26/27
- Analyses the development and stability of German democracy in comparative perspective, linking political theory to institutional evolution and contemporary challenges.


## Small States in the International System: A Comparative Perspective

- SoSe 2022, 2023, and 2024
- Examines how small states operate within the international system, focusing on the constraints they face and the strategies they use to exert influence despite limited resources.
